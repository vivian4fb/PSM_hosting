About

What this is. An interactive companion to Slanted planar volume gratings: parallel-stacked-mirror models versus traditional coupled wave descriptions by David Brotherton-Ratcliffe and Vivian Amos Sureshkumar (2026). The sliders set a planar grating — mean index, thickness, recording geometry, slant Ψ, index modulation n₁ and Kogelnik's optical densities D₀, D₁ — and the panel plots the diffraction efficiency across the replay band for four two-wave descriptions next to a rigorous coupled-wave (RCWA) solution of the same boundary-value problem.

Independence. This is an independent implementation written from the paper's equations and validated against the values printed in it: the twelve figure configurations reachable from the “Paper figures” menu reproduce the rms departures quoted in the paper's Figs. 6, 9, 12, 17, 20 and 23 to the printed precision (see the “paper” column in the table above after pressing Verify at L = 10). The authors' own code deposit (release v1.1) is a separate code base.

Conventions. Angles θc, θr are measured from the directed fringe normal; the slant Ψ = 0 is an unslanted reflection grating and Ψ = 90° an unslanted transmission grating; a slant is classified reflection or transmission by the sign of cSK (eq. 46). In family mode the plate is replayed by its own recording beam: θc = Ψ + θin, θr = θc, Bragg at λr. The optical densities follow eq. (175), χ = D λc cos(θc − Ψ)/(2πd), the density accumulated along the reference ray. The rigorous reference embeds the grating in an index-matched lossless exterior (Appendix A.1), so no curve carries a plate-surface Fresnel term. At Ψ = 0 exactly the rigorous curve is the chain-matrix reflectance of Appendix A.6.

The four models. PSM: the parallel-stacked-mirror model as published (Brotherton-Ratcliffe, J. Mod. Opt. 2012; Appl. Opt. 2015), extended here to slant, loss and both polarisations. PSM*: the same construction with its one avoidable assumption removed — the coupling constant carries the exact factor ζ = cos θc/(α cos θr), unity at Bragg (paper Section 5). Kogelnik: the classical coupled-wave theory (Bell Syst. Tech. J. 1969). TCWT: a two-wave truncation of the exact harmonic-index equations, which differs from Kogelnik by the term iκ²/β in σ and by the obliquity factor in π (Section 6).

How the rigorous curve is computed. The Python reference module psmgrating/rcwa.py runs unchanged inside your browser through Pyodide (Floquet expansion |l| ≤ L, dense eigen-solve, eigenmode anchoring). A 301-point preview at L = 4 appears first; Verify at L = 10 recomputes the full grid at the paper's production truncation. Nothing leaves your machine.
